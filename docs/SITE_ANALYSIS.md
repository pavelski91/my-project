Scale calibration: at 200 dpi and 1:500 the nominal pixel size is 0.0635 m; the traced polygon then measures 1357 m², i.e. 4.4 % low. A scale of 0.0649 m/px reproduces the stated 1418 m². The dimension chain printed on the plan (5.00 + 4.50 + 15.60 + 4.50 + 6.32) relates to the dwelling/service layout, not to a boundary edge, and was not used as a scale reference.

Reconstructed objects: left dwelling (x 5.2–9.4, z 12.7–20.1), right dwelling footprint (x 25.2–29.5, z 16.6–24.0), tin garage `g1` (x 2.0–5.4, z 22.2–26.8), water/sewer/electric routes, waste point, hard-standing at the entrance, 17 spot heights from 148.8 m (north-west) to 145.4 m (south).

Terrain: plane fit through the spot heights plus an inverse-distance-weighted residual, levelled to 148.10 m under the left dwelling. The land falls ≈ 3 m from the road (north) to the south-east corner (lowest point, 145.4–146.5 m) — about 8–9 %. That is why the boardwalk is built on piles that get taller downhill, and why the rain garden and pond sit in the south-east.

## What could not be extracted (and how it is handled)

* **True north.** The sheet is assumed north-up. A *sheet bearing* setting (±30°) rotates the sun path.
* **Which dwelling is "yours".** The plan has two dwelling footprints; the user's existing house is the left one. The right one is kept as a *reserved footprint* and can be released in Settings.
* **Setbacks.** Only the road/east building lines are visible and they lie outside the parcel. A conservative 3.0 m (habitable pavilions) / 1.0 m (decks, features) is used as a *guide* and always flagged "verify".
* **Existing vegetation, soil, groundwater.** Not on the plan → treated as unknown. The plot is treated as pasture.
