# House for a family of four — architect's concept

Constraints taken as fixed: the existing house (4.2 × 7.4 m, built), the poured slab for the extension (4.2 × 3.3 m), WZ limits (≤ 70 m² built area, ≤ 120 m² floor area, ≤ 2 storeys, ≤ 8 m, gable 30–45°).

| Zone | Where | Area (net, approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Parents' bedroom + wardrobe | extension (poured slab) | 10 m² |
| Family bathroom (bath/shower, WC, washer) + corridor | existing house, north | 5 m² + 3 m² |
| Entrance hall under the loft (bench, wardrobe wall, hooks) | existing house, west door | 5 m² |
| Study / kids' day room (two desks, shelves, daybed — also a guest bed) | existing house, south | 12 m² |
| **Family annex: kitchen + dining (6) + living with sofa and stove** | new wing, east | 22 m² |
| **Kids' sleeping loft** (two beds, play space) over the bedroom, bathroom and hall | mezzanine | ≈ 22 m² |
| Reading nook over the bathroom | mezzanine | 6 m² |

Why this works: the heavy living functions get their own full-height volume with the big glazing, terrace and loggia (the annex), while the existing small house keeps the quiet functions. The existing envelope (eaves 2.9 m) is too low for rooms in the roof, so the children sleep on the mezzanine, which is how the supplied section is designed anyway. The study can become a third bedroom later.

Footprint: 31.1 + 13.9 + 24.8 = **69.8 m²** (limit 70). Floor area ≈ 70 + 22 + 6 ≈ 98 m² (limit 120). Parking: 2-car carport (WZ: 2–4 spaces).

To be confirmed by the architect: the roof valley between the two gables, structure over the wide opening in the old east wall, loft headroom and balustrade heights, fire separation of the stove, and whether the boiler/heat pump goes in the workshop (WZ allows boiler room, heat pump or electric heating).
